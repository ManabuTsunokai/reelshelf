## 1\. Identify the tape format and check its condition

Begin by identifying the tape format and confirming that you have a device capable of playing it. Common consumer videotape formats include the following.

| Tape | Typical playback device |
| --- | --- |
| VHS | VHS VCR |
| VHS-C | VHS-C camcorder, or a VHS VCR with a cassette adapter |
| Video8 or Hi8 | 8mm camcorder that supports the recorded format |
| Digital8 | Digital8 camcorder |
| MiniDV | MiniDV camcorder |

Tapes that look alike do not necessarily use the same recording format. For example, many Digital8 camcorders can also play Video8 and Hi8 recordings, but some models cannot. Check the model numbers of the tape and playback device, then consult the device manual to confirm compatibility.

Inspect the tape and cassette before playing it. Do not play a tape if you notice a white powder or fuzzy growth, severe warping, broken tape, or water damage. Mold can contaminate the playback device and may also present a health risk. A tape in this condition is safer in the hands of a professional media-preservation or transfer service.

If the tape looks normal, play it only briefly at first. Check that both picture and sound are present, that the tape does not stop, and that the player does not make unusual noises. Playback equipment can also deteriorate after years without use, so do not continue if it behaves unpredictably.

## 2\. Prepare the equipment

To transfer an analog recording such as VHS, VHS-C, Video8, or Hi8 to a computer, you will generally need:

-   A VCR or camcorder that can play the tape
-   A USB video capture device
-   A computer

A USB video capture device converts the analog video and audio from a VCR or camcorder into data that recording software can save on a computer. Examples include:

-   [Elgato Video Capture](https://www.elgato.com/us/en/p/video-capture): Supports Windows and macOS, accepts composite and S-Video input, and captures video as H.264 using its companion software.
-   [Roxio Easy VHS to DVD 3 Plus](https://www.roxio.com/en/products/easy-vhs-to-dvd/standard/): A capture device and Windows software package for converting VHS and camcorder recordings into digital video.

Product availability and operating-system support vary by country. Before buying a capture device, confirm that it supports your computer, the outputs on your VCR or camcorder, and the video standard used by the tape. File formats, bundled cables, and recording software also differ between products. An HDMI-only capture device cannot connect directly to an older player that only has yellow, red, and white analog outputs.

In a typical composite connection, yellow carries the video signal, while red and white carry the right and left audio channels. If both the player and capture device support S-Video, you can use S-Video for the picture and the red and white connectors for sound.

The connection looks like this:

```text
VCR or camcorder
    ↓ Video and audio cables
USB video capture device
    ↓ USB
Computer
```

MiniDV and Digital8 are digital formats. With a compatible camcorder, computer, and capture application, they can be transferred over i.LINK, also known as IEEE 1394 or FireWire. Most current computers no longer include an i.LINK port, however, and older bundled applications may not work on current operating systems. When a suitable digital-transfer setup is unavailable, another option is to connect the camcorder's analog output to a USB video capture device.

A USB port on a camcorder does not necessarily support transferring recordings from tape. Check the camcorder manual for the supported method of importing tape footage to a computer.

## 3\. Capture the home video on your computer

### Connect the player and computer

If the capture device requires a driver or recording application, install it first by following the manufacturer's instructions. Connect the video and audio outputs from the player to the USB video capture device, then connect the capture device to a USB port on the computer.

Open the recording application and select the USB capture device as the video and audio source. Select the standard used by the original recording, such as NTSC, PAL, or SECAM. If you are unsure, check the camcorder, VCR, tape label, or device manual. Most older home-video recordings use a 4:3 aspect ratio. Match the setting to the source so the picture is not stretched horizontally to 16:9.

## 4\. Choose a video file format

If the recording application lets you choose the output format, the following combination is widely supported by browsers and devices for everyday viewing.

```text
Container: MP4
Video: H.264 (AVC)
Audio: AAC
```

If the recording application does not offer this combination, you can capture the video first and convert it with another application afterward. See “[Convert and upload a video](/en/help/convert-and-upload/)” for conversion instructions.

A higher quality setting can preserve more of the original picture but also produces a larger file. Stronger compression makes the file smaller at the cost of image detail.

## 5\. When digitizing the tapes yourself is difficult

Consider using a professional transfer service instead of playing the tape yourself when:

-   You cannot find compatible playback equipment
-   The tape has mold, warping, a break, or water damage
-   The player makes unusual noises or the tape stops
-   You have too many tapes to capture in the time available

Look for a local provider using terms such as `video tape digitization service`, `VHS to MP4 service`, `MiniDV transfer service`, or `Hi8 to digital service`.

Supported tape formats, delivery methods, prices, and damaged-tape services differ between providers. If you want to use the digitized videos on a computer or in the cloud, make sure the provider can deliver MP4 or another video file rather than only a DVD. Also check the turnaround time, how the original tapes will be returned, how long uploaded files are retained, when working copies are deleted, whether any part of the work is subcontracted, and how the provider protects your privacy.

## 6\. Make your digitized home videos easy to watch

If a digitized video exists only on one computer or external drive, you can generally watch it only on a computer connected to that storage. Watching it on a phone or another computer means moving the file or reconnecting the drive.

Computers and external drives can also fail. If you do not keep and maintain another copy yourself, you may lose access to the videos you took the time to digitize.

A cloud service is a convenient option when you want to keep home videos safe and watch them across locations and devices. “[Four ways to access your videos from anywhere](/en/blog/access-your-videos-anywhere/)” compares cloud storage, self-hosted media servers, video platforms, and Tamareel.

[Tamareel](/en/) lets you build your own private video library in the cloud. Upload your digitized home videos and watch them in a computer or phone browser whenever you want. You can add titles, descriptions, and tags; group recordings into series and playlists by year or event; and create edited videos that play selected parts of a longer recording without changing the source video.

Tamareel does not include video-sharing features. It is designed as a private library for people who want to **organize their memories and return to them whenever they choose**, rather than publish their home videos for others.

## Summary

To digitize old home videos yourself, use a compatible VCR or camcorder and a USB video capture device to record the playback on your computer. For MiniDV and Digital8, a direct i.LINK transfer may also be available if you have compatible equipment and software.

After capture, check the complete file and prepare a viewing copy in a broadly supported format such as MP4 with H.264 video and AAC audio. Storing that copy in the cloud makes it easier to watch from a computer or phone that is not connected to the original drive.
