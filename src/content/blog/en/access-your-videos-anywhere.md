## Four options

| Approach | Main purpose | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cloud storage | Storing, syncing, and sharing files | People who want to manage documents and photos alongside their videos |
| Video platform | Playing, publishing, and sharing videos | People who want to show videos to others |
| Self-hosted server | Building your own video library | People who enjoy managing hardware and configuring systems |
| Tamareel | Building your own video library | People who want an easy-to-browse personal library without running a server |

## 1\. Cloud storage: Keep videos alongside all your other files

Google Drive, OneDrive, iCloud Drive, and Dropbox are services that let you store files—including videos—in the cloud and access them from multiple devices. You can organize files into folders and share them with links or invitations, making cloud storage a good fit when you also want to manage documents, photos, and other files.

Most cloud storage services can play videos, but they are designed first and foremost to store and share many kinds of files. As a result, video playback, thumbnail previews, and organization using metadata such as recording dates and tags are generally less developed than in a dedicated video service. The usual workflow is to find a video by its folder or filename and open it, which can make a growing collection harder to **browse, choose from, and revisit**.

## 2\. Video platforms: Use a service designed for sharing

YouTube and Vimeo let you upload, play, and share videos. They handle encoding, video players, and viewing pages for you, making it easy to present work, build a portfolio, share instructions with clients, or send videos to family and friends.

Be sure to understand the visibility settings. YouTube offers public, unlisted, and private videos. Unlisted videos do not appear in search results, but anyone with the link can share it again. Vimeo offers options such as public, unlisted, password-protected, embed-only, and private, although the available choices depend on the plan. Both are convenient services built around showing videos to other people. Even when a video is private, it can still be subject to the service's terms and content review processes.

## 3\. Self-hosted server: Build your own Netflix-like setup

Plex and Jellyfin turn videos stored on a computer, NAS, or home server into a media library that you can watch through apps on a television, phone, or computer. The ability to browse a polished library and enjoy it on a large screen is appealing, and this option comes close to the idea of building your own Netflix.

That flexibility comes with responsibility for the storage hardware, uptime, and maintenance. To watch away from home, a Plex server must be powered on, connected to the internet, and configured for remote access. Your connection's upload speed and the server's ability to convert videos for different devices also affect the viewing experience. Depending on your circumstances, remote playback with Plex may require a paid plan.

## 4\. Tamareel: Turn your videos into a library you will revisit

Tamareel is designed for building **your own private video library**. Upload videos, organize them into series and playlists, and watch them in a browser. Built-in video editing also lets you remove unwanted sections from unedited footage and keep each video in the form you want to remember it.

Tamareel does not include publishing or sharing features, so there is no risk of accidentally selecting the wrong public visibility setting. It is a good fit when you want your videos to feel like a browsable collection of works and memories rather than a list of filenames.

## How to choose

- **Store and sync every kind of file, including videos** → Cloud storage
- **Show or embed creative work and instructional videos** → A video platform such as YouTube or Vimeo
- **Choose your own hardware and build a television-focused media setup** → A self-hosted server such as Plex or Jellyfin
- **Organize your own videos and revisit them easily, without maintaining a server** → Tamareel

Whichever option you choose, keep a separate backup so that you do not lose important videos. The place you use for convenient viewing does not have to be the same place where you preserve the original files.
